      Platform notes

        Tool support changes with ECU software versions. Confirm your exact car and ECU version with the tuner before paying.

        How the file gets on the car

        OBD flash

        Written through the diagnostic port in 10–30 minutes. Quick and reversible if the tuner saves your original file.

        Bench / boot mode

        The ECU is removed and opened to read or unlock it. Needed on many newer protected ECUs. Takes longer and must be done carefully to avoid damage.

        Handheld flasher

        A device or app you own (e.g. Accessport, FlashPro, bootmod3 or MHD-style tools) that stores the stock file and lets you switch maps and data-log yourself.

        05 · How to choose

        Choosing mods in the right order

        The most common regret on owners' forums is buying power first. This is the order experienced owners and UK tuners recommend.

        Decide the car's job

        Daily through Glasgow traffic and speed bumps, weekend runs up the A82 and A83, Knockhill days, or meets. A car set up for one does the others worse. Write the job down and test every purchase against it.

        Prove it's healthy

        Get a baseline dyno run, a data log, a boost-leak test and a look at service history. Tuning a tired engine finds its weak point faster. Fix faults first; an engine warning light is an MOT fail on its own.

        Phone your insurer first

        Get quotes for the finished build before buying parts. Some insurers refuse remaps or stage 2 outright. Everything that differs from factory spec must be declared, including mods a previous owner fitted.

        Tyres, brakes, alignment

        Quality tyres, high-temperature fluid, better pads and a fast-road alignment change more on a wet B-road than 40PS. They're cheap, reversible and make every later mod work better.

        Chassis before power

        Dampers, anti-roll bars and bushes let you use what the car already has. Keep the ride height sensible: Scottish roads, potholes and city speed humps punish slammed cars.

        Add power in stages

        Stage 1 map on a stock car, then supporting hardware (intercooler, intake, downpipe with a high-flow cat) for stage 2. Log after each change. Budget for clutch, gearbox software and cooling as power rises.

        Choose the tuner carefully

        Use a business with a dyno that shows before-and-after graphs, data-logs the car, can put it back to stock, and refuses emissions deletes. If a garage advertises DPF, cat or EGR removal, go elsewhere.

        Record, declare, maintain

        Keep receipts and dyno sheets, update your insurer the day a mod is fitted, shorten oil-change intervals on tuned engines, and tell DVLA about changes it lists, such as colour, engine or body.

        Picking a tuner

        Questions to ask

        • Can I see before-and-after dyno graphs from a car like mine, on your dyno?
        • Is the map custom to my car, or a tuned off-the-shelf file, and what AFR, timing and boost targets does it run?
        • Will you data-log on the road after mapping, and can you return the car to the original software?
        • Does the map need a gearbox (TCU) update, and is that included?
        • What do you recommend changing on service intervals, plugs and oil grade?
        • Is your work covered by traders' insurance, and what happens if something fails?

        Walk away if they

        • Offer DPF, GPF, catalyst, EGR or AdBlue removal "for off-road use"
        • Quote peak power without showing a graph or data log
        • Say a mod is "MOT-proof" or doesn't need declaring

        Visual guide · window tint

        Will your front tint pass?

        UK law measures the glass and film together. Most modern glass already blocks some light, so a "70%" film on factory glass usually ends up well under 70%.

        Fails

        Front side windows need at least 70% combined light transmission.

        06 · Scotland & the law

        Glasgow rules of the road

        What actually gets modified cars stopped, failed or uninsured in Scotland, plus where to measure and use the power legally.

        Glasgow Low Emission Zone

        Quick LEZ check

        Enforced since 1 June 2023 by number-plate cameras across the city centre inside the M8, the Clyde and High Street. This is a rule-of-thumb check; confirm with the official checker.

        Penalty starts at £60 (£30 if paid within 14 days) and doubles for repeat breaches up to £480 for a car. There is no daily charge: non-compliant cars simply may not enter. Official checker: glasgow.gov.uk/LEZ

        Measure it, then use it

        Dynos & track

        Knockhill Racing CircuitFife · ~1 hr from Glasgow

        Scotland's national circuit. Open-pitlane car track days and newcomer track nights. Strict 105dB static noise limit, so check a loud exhaust before you book.

        Dyna TuneEast Kilbride

        Dynapack hub dyno, remapping and diagnostics, around 30 minutes from the city centre.

        Autotec ScotlandInverclyde

        4WD Dyno Developments rolling road and laser alignment ramps; recommended for Greenock and Port Glasgow owners on local forums.

        Star PerformanceGlenrothes, Fife

        Long-standing Scottish rolling road often recommended on marque forums for consistent power runs.

        Listed because owners recommend them on PistonHeads and marque forums, not as an endorsement. See the screened garage list for review data. Check they're still trading, read recent reviews and ask the tuner questions above.

        Emissions equipment

        A car that had a catalyst or particulate filter from new fails the MOT if it's missing, and using it on the road is already an offence under the Construction and Use Regulations. The DfT consulted until 6 September 2026 on making DPF/GPF removal, cat removal, AdBlue and EGR tampering explicit offences with fines from £1,000.

        DVSA · DfT in-use emissions consultation, 2026

        Insurance

        Anything that differs from factory spec is a modification and must be declared, including work done by a previous owner. The Financial Ombudsman has warned that undeclared mods lead to voided policies and rejected claims.

        Financial Ombudsman Service, via Which?

        Headlights

        LED bulbs in halogen headlamp units fail the MOT on cars first used from 1 April 1986 ("light source and lamp not compatible"), whatever the packaging says. Only complete LED headlamp units approved for LED are legal.

        DVSA MOT inspection manual 4.1.4

        Window tint

        From 1 April 1985 on: windscreen at least 75% light through, front side windows at least 70%. Rear glass has no limit. Police and DVSA measure it and can issue a prohibition notice.

        GOV.UK tinted vehicle windows

        Noise & anti-social driving

        Exhausts must not be louder than when the car was built or have been altered to increase noise. In Scotland, the Antisocial Behaviour etc. (Scotland) Act 2004 lets police seize a car used in a way that causes alarm or annoyance after a warning. Crackle maps and revving at meets are the usual trigger.

        Construction & Use Regs · ASB (Scotland) Act 2004 s.126

        Telling DVLA

        A remap doesn't go on your V5C, but DVLA must be told about changes it lists, including colour (a full-colour wrap counts), engine or cylinder capacity, fuel type, chassis or body changes and seating.

        GOV.UK change vehicle details

        Scottish weather

        Winter salt eats aftermarket fasteners, braided-line fittings and bare exhausts. Use stainless hardware, underbody wax and a dedicated winter or all-season tyre set; summer tyres like the Pilot Sport 4S lose grip sharply below about 7°C.

        Tyre manufacturer guidance · owner experience

        Fuel

        Tuners map for 99 RON (Shell V-Power, Tesco Momentum 99, Esso Synergy Supreme+ 99). Filling with 95 RON on a stage 2 map invites knock and pulled timing. E85 is not sold at UK forecourts, so flex-fuel builds are mostly for enthusiasts with their own supply.

        UK tuner guidance

        07 · Garages near Glasgow

        Garages, screened

        Trustpilot scores checked on 30 September 2026, plus our own screen: any business that advertises emissions deletes (DPF, GPF, EGR, AdBlue or catalyst removal) is flagged, because that work is illegal on the road and a sign of how the business operates.

        Reading the scores

        Why most local tuners have no Trustpilot score

        • Small independent Scottish tuners mostly collect Google and Facebook reviews, not Trustpilot. "No profile" isn't a bad sign on its own.
        • Under about 20 reviews, one angry or glowing review swings the score. Check how recent the reviews are, too.
        • National networks (Quantum, chains) have one score for hundreds of branches. Your local branch may be better or worse.
        • Profiles that are unclaimed or don't invite reviews may not be representative, as Trustpilot itself notes.
        • Cross-check with owners' clubs and forums for your model, and ask to see dyno graphs from cars like yours.

        Garage services to book, in order

        What to use each type of garage for

        • Tyre and alignment centres: performance tyres, four-wheel alignment after suspension work, MOT.
        • Performance specialist with a dyno: baseline power run, remap, gearbox software, data-logging, stage 2 hardware.
        • Marque specialist or main dealer: warranty work, recalls, software updates on newer cars.
        • Security installer (Thatcham-approved): trackers and CAN-bus immobilisers, with an installation certificate for your insurer.
